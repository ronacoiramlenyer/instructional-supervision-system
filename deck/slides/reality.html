Problem · Your job as coordinator, without a system

Manual records. Isolated tools. Feedback that can’t diagnose.

Fragmented data

CM, LG and observation checks recorded by hand, each in its own format.

Hidden trends

Tracking growth meant re-reading records. Training needs stayed invisible.

Weak merit basis

Compiling evidence was slow; feedback rarely said what to fix.

(~50 sec) Keep them in the role: “So, coordinator — here’s what you’re working with today.” This is RQ1 — the challenges in our current process at LSGH. Walk the three cards: 1. Fragmented data — coordinators do monitor CMs, LGs and observations, but recording is manual and every supervisor devises their own approach and format. 2. Hidden trends — to see how a teacher performs over time, you have to thematically re-read records. Training needs are hard to pinpoint. 3. Weak merit basis — compiling for merit rating is slow, and feedback to teachers is generally not diagnostic. Transition: “So the question became: can we build the coordinator a system that actually answers that prompt — with evidence?” Then name the study (the title is on the cover, so say it, don’t show it again): “That is my study — the Instructional Supervision System: a data-driven, automated system for monitoring and evaluating teachers’ performance.”
