(~50 sec) Now the results, starting with RQ1: what challenges affect recording, monitoring and sharing teacher performance data? The first three came from the manual process we started with: records fragmented across supervisors and formats; recording and compiling done by hand; and no way to follow a teacher’s performance over a long time span. The last three surfaced during the DBR cycles themselves, and are documented in the appendices: - Raw counts and equal weights let teachers score high while missing critical indicators (Cycle 2, Appendix H). - Teachers who co-wrote one CM or LG had to be encoded again and again, and entries were overwritten (Cycle 5, Appendix G). - Feedback still had to be obtained, interpreted and delivered by hand, which delayed it (Cycle 6). The right column is the answer to each — and that sets up RQ2 to RQ4: the design, the output, and the impact.
