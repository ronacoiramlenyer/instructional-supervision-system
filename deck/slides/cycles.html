Solution · RQ2 · Design-Based Research

Six cycles of use and refinement

CYCLE 1

Indicator checklist

Auto-feedback on unmet indicators; raw counts only

CYCLE 2

Scores and radars

Normalized scale; weights so critical indicators count

CYCLE 3

Analytics, extended

Indicator-level percentages; CM model applied to LG, COT

CYCLE 4

Data integrity

Per-indicator storage and validation checks

CYCLE 5

Shared scoring

One score set for co-written maps and guides

CYCLE 6

Decision support

Auto-emailed reports; department dashboards

Version 3.0, after three years of use, now feeds the school’s merit rating

(~75 sec) Method: Design-Based Research — design, use it in the real setting, evaluate, refine. Feedback came from academic team meetings, department discussions and post-conferences with teachers. Each cycle fixed a problem the previous one exposed: 1. A checklist with automatic feedback — but raw counts couldn’t be compared. 2. A standardized score and radar charts. We learned equal weights let teachers score high while missing critical indicators, so we added grouped weighting. 3. Indicator-level percentages, and the model was extended from CM to LG to COT. 4. Database rebuilt to store every indicator, with validation, so longitudinal reports are possible. 5. Shared scoring — teachers often co-write one CM; before, encoding a second teacher erased the first. 6. Automatic emailing and department dashboards — from a reporting tool to a decision-support tool. By year 3, version 3.0 was integrated into merit rating.
