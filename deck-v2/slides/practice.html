Key contribution: a school can grow its own decision-support system by answering real operational needs.

[~8:20] Beyond these numerical results, the implementation also demonstrated practical improvements in instructional supervision. Coordinators were able to identify strengths and areas for improvement across teachers and departments, develop more targeted action plans, and use performance information to guide professional development discussions. The system also facilitated more timely feedback because reports could be automatically generated and distributed after evaluation. An important point I would like to emphasize is that the system was not developed overnight. Its effectiveness came from continuous refinement based on actual use. Across six development cycles, improvements were made to scoring, visualization, indicator-level analysis, data integrity, collaborative evaluation, and automated report distribution. This is one of the key contributions of the study: demonstrating how an educational institution can progressively develop a decision-support system by responding to its actual operational needs.
